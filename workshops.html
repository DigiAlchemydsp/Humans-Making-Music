Sound design

Start with one sound.

Make a single patch carry the track before you arrange anything.

Pick one source, shape it with a filter and a short envelope, then listen to what the room does to it. A sound that works alone rarely needs much company.

We look at layering, noise as texture, and the small movements in pitch and level that keep a loop alive. Then we record the result as audio, so the part becomes a fixed decision instead of another preset to browse.

Advanced techniques

Make the take do more.

Turn a clean pattern into a performance.

Macros, conditional triggers and hand-played automation let a loop breathe without adding more elements. One good part, played with intent, holds a room longer than five safe ones.

We work through resampling, controlled feedback and ways to keep a hybrid setup repeatable. The goal is a performance you can repeat and a recording you can edit.

Studio routing

Follow the signal.

Know every stage between the instrument and the speakers.

Gain staging comes first. Set levels at the source, leave headroom at each stage, and label the path from instrument to interface to monitors.

We cover patchbays, sends and returns, latency compensation and clocking for hybrid rigs. A routing plan drawn on paper saves hours in the room.

Mastering

Translate everywhere.

A master that holds up on small speakers and headphones.

Mastering starts with a mix that already balances itself. We compare against reference tracks, watch the low end, and test on small speakers before touching the limiter.

We look at loudness targets for streaming, sequencing, and how to deliver files with the right headroom. A quiet master that translates beats a loud one that does not.
